On emotion, obsession, and the second skin you choose.

The Origin

There are feelings that arrive before you have words for them. I spent years looking for the right container — and found it wasn't a sentence. It was a scent. This began with an ache, persistent and quiet, that clung to the edges of memory like the scent of damp earth after a summer storm, mixed with the faint sweetness of night-blooming jasmine. It wasn't sadness, not quite nostalgia, but a sensation of wanting something so intensely it felt like a physical absence. A yearning for a version of comfort that was always just slightly out of reach, or a connection that felt profoundly understood but also fragile, fleeting. This feeling crystallized around certain people who moved through my life like ghosts, leaving trails of scents I couldn't quite grasp — old paper, cold stone, a specific type of incense — alongside the profound sense that something vital had passed without ever truly being held.

There were so many feelings that existed outside the easy categories, emotions that needed a home and deserved to feel as important as the good ones. I often felt like I was living in a monochrome world, constantly trying to force vibrant internal landscapes into flat, manageable concepts. The everyday language available to me offered no true container for the unspeakable. It demanded I dissect and define, when what I truly yearned for was simply to feel. This ache remained, a quiet hum beneath the surface, growing louder, more insistent, until the structures I'd built to understand my own inner life felt like a cage. This inability to fully honor the spectrum of emotion was precisely what pushed me towards scent.

The Philosophy

"Quiéreme tal y como soy" — Love me just as I am. This wasn't a slogan I invented for a brand; it was a quiet prayer I needed to whisper to myself, a phrase that finally started to heal something. For years, I edited myself, softened my edges, tried to become palatable, believing my authentic self was too much to be loved, too messy, too intense. I needed to hear it from that younger version of myself, and then from the parts of me that still, occasionally, flinch from judgment. This phrase is not a demand; it's an invitation, an acceptance. It's permission to exist in all your difficult, contradictory truth. It's for anyone who has ever felt like an excess, or a lack, in someone else's estimation. It's a whisper back to the parts of myself I tried to hide, and a promise to honor them, fiercely.

How We Work

My creative process at Quiéreme doesn't begin with a list of notes; it begins with an emotion, raw and undefined. Take Ciegamente. That fragrance didn't start with rum and tobacco. It started with the feeling of a consuming, irrational obsession — the kind that makes you blind to everything else, a singular focus bordering on madness. How does that feel in the body? A dizzying pull, a dark warmth, a sense of being consumed. I immerse myself in that visceral experience, letting it guide me. Then, and only then, do I seek out the olfactory echoes: the heady, almost aggressive sweetness of dark rum, the deep, earthy smoke of tobacco, the intoxicating pull of patchouli. It's a reverse engineering of the soul, capturing the emotional raw material first, then chasing down its aromatic shadow.

I want someone to put on a Quiéreme fragrance and recognise a part of themselves they hadn't given voice to. Not to become someone else — but to be seen, fully, as who they already are.

A mirror. A permission slip. A quiet, insistent whisper that you are enough, exactly as you are, right now.
